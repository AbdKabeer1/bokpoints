1. Summary
BOKPOINTS is a loyalty platform that gives small and medium-sized businesses (SMEs)
the same retention tools that large chains have: points, rewards, discounts and customer
alerts. Customers earn points when a cashier scans them at purchase, and they see their
balance for every participating store in one place.
The product has three surfaces: a customer dashboard, a store dashboard and an admin
panel. Stores pay a monthly subscription. Customers use the product for free. The first
market is Finland / EU.
2. Problem and opportunity
Big chains win repeat customers through loyalty programs, targeted offers and
notifications. Small businesses lack the budget and technology to do the same, so they
lose returning customers to chains that reward them.
Customers feel this too. Many favor stores that give rewards over independent shops that
do not, even when they would prefer the independent shop.
Opportunity: give SMEs a ready-made loyalty and marketing tool at a monthly price they
can afford, and give customers one wallet that collects rewards across the local shops
they already use.
3. Vision and positioning
BOKPOINTS is a B2B2C product.
Business customer (pays): the SME, which gets a loyalty program and a marketing
channel to its existing customers.
End user (free): the shopper, who earns and tracks points across stores.

4. Users and roles
A customer has one global account (identified by a verified email address, with duplicates
prevented). Stores link customers to their store; they do not own separate accounts for
them.
5. Business model
Revenue: monthly subscription paid by each store.
Customers: free.
Failed payments: reminder to the owner at day 7, store paused at day 14 (campaigns,
rewards and rules locked, awarding continues), store suspended at day 30 (awarding
blocked). Customers keep access to their balances throughout.
Pricing details: not decided yet (see open questions). Candidates include flat tiers by
feature set, or tiers by number of active customers.

6. Goals and non-goals
Goals
1. Let a cashier award points to a customer in a few seconds at the till.
2. Give customers a single view of their points at every participating store.
3. Give stores tools to manage customers, rewards, discounted products and customer
alerts without technical skills.
4. Give the admin full control to onboard and support stores.
5. Design the system so that new identification methods (NFC card, wallet passes) and
POS integrations can be added without rewriting the core.
Non-goals (for now)
A universal points currency usable across stores. Points are per store.
Transferring or gifting points, and gift cards (planned for a later phase).
Acting as a payment instrument or processing customer payments.
A marketplace or online shop for customers to buy from stores.
Native POS integrations in the MVP.

9. Product requirements overview
This section states what each surface must do. Detailed flows, rules and data are in the
FRD.
9.1 Customer dashboard
Sign up and sign in with one account, verified by email. Available on the Apple App
Store, Google Play and the web.
Personal QR code for identification at the till, which also works offline.
See points balance per store and the history of points earned, redeemed and expired,
with a notice for points about to expire.
See rewards and offers from stores where the customer has points (v1.1).
Manage notification and marketing preferences.
Later: alerts about sales, gifting or transferring points, gift cards.
9.2 Store dashboard
Manage the store profile, staff accounts with role permissions, and cashier devices.
Define the points rules (earn rate, minimum purchase, maximum per transaction).
Points expire 3 months after they are earned, for every store.
Cashier view, installable on a phone or tablet, to scan a customer, enter the amount and
award points, online or offline. Redeeming points comes in v1.1.

9.4 Cross-cutting requirements
Credential model: a customer can have several credentials (app QR code, NFC card,
later wallet passes). All of them resolve to the same customer ID.
API-first: the "award points" action is an API call used by the cashier screen today and
by POS integrations later.
Offline-capable awarding: the cashier device can check the QR code without a
connection. Awards are queued on the device and synced later, and the server
validates them at sync.
Points lifecycle: points expire 3 months after they are earned. There are no reversals,
and a balance never goes below zero except through an admin fraud clawback.
Audit log: every points change records who, when, which store and why.
Fraud controls: only staff can award points, awards use the entered purchase amount,
offline awards are limited and checked at sync, and unusual activity can be limited or
flagged.
Localization: Finnish and English at launch (proposed), euro currency, Finnish VAT on
invoices. Other EU languages later.
Security: secure authentication, role-based permissions, encrypted data in transit and
at rest, and encrypted offline data on cashier devices.

11. Identification and POS strategy
1. MVP: QR code in the customer app, scanned by the cashier with a phone or tablet
camera. Amount entered by hand. The code can be checked offline.
2. v1.1: physical NFC card that holds only an opaque ID (never a balance), with
cryptographic protection to prevent cloning. Cashiers need an NFC-capable device or
reader.
3. v2: Apple and Google Wallet passes. A pass that works by tapping on Apple devices
depends on Apple's NFC entitlement, which Apple grants at its discretion, and on
terminals that support Apple's Value Added Services protocol. A wallet pass showing a
QR code is a simpler first step.
4. POS integrations: exposed through the same API, one vendor at a time, based on
which POS systems the first stores use.
The loyalty card is an identifier, not a payment card.
12. Market and compliance (Finland / EU)
This is a planning note, not legal advice. Review before launch.
GDPR: customer data is personal data. Define the lawful basis, data minimization,
retention, access and deletion rights, and the roles of BOKPOINTS and stores as
controllers or processors. Stores see only their own customers' data.
Marketing messages: alerts and promotions to customers need clear consent and an
easy way to opt out. Check the Finnish and EU rules on electronic direct marketing.
Points terms: points have no cash value; state the 3-month expiry and the redemption
rules in the terms.
Gift cards and transferable value (v2): may raise financial-regulation questions.
Review before building.
Subscription billing: invoices and VAT handled correctly for Finnish businesses.
Language: Finnish and English support at launch.
Pending customers: unclaimed pending-customer records, including the email
address, are kept for 1 year and then deleted. The lawful basis for keeping them needs
a privacy review.

15. Open questions
1. Subscription pricing: tiers, free trial, setup fee, and whether NFC cards are charged
separately.
2. Points rules: standard earn rates and the redemption options for v1.1. Expiry is decided
(3 months).
3. Technology stack and hosting: cross-platform customer apps, an offline-capable web
app for cashiers, and sync (needed for AGENT.md).
4. Offline limits and security: the cap per offline award, the queue size and the credential
validity period (see the FRD).
5. Which POS systems the pilot stores use.
6. Pilot area and the first stores to approach.
7. Brand, name checks and domain for the Finnish and EU market.
8. Who handles tax and legal review for terms of service and privacy policy, including how
SMS costs are handled later.
